## Engine decision and comparison

**Choose Rapier compat 0.19.3 (Apache-2.0) for the production world core**, lazy-loaded when the first solid is unlocked. The cannon-es mousepick example supplies the right interaction architecture, and the working cannon spike demonstrates it, but measured 20-prop contact cost rejects cannon as our production default: 3.20–3.41 ms/120 Hz step versus Rapier's 0.27–0.32 ms with the same fitted shapes and CCD enabled. Accept Rapier's larger payload to buy contact headroom and narrow-object protection; do not load it before the cat. Jolt also performs well in the box screening test, but adds more payload and manual lifetime management. A homegrown general solver offers no measured advantage and would put contact-manifold and joint correctness on us.

**Prototype/production distinction:** `index.html` in the spike deliberately remains the tested cannon implementation, not a claim that Rapier has already been integrated into the page. `research.mjs` reconstructs its exact 20 compound shapes, transforms and masses in Rapier and records the matched workload separately. This makes the failed cannon budget reviewable instead of concealing it behind the tiny two-prop demo. Unit 1 ports the demonstrated grab/sleep contract to Rapier and verifies pointer joints plus full render cost on hardware. Rapier's actual iPhone budget and joint behavior are still gates, not established by the CPU comparison.

### Measurement method and limits

`engines.html` runs 20 equal boxes falling into contact with a floor at 1/120 s, 60 warm-up steps and 600 measured steps, sleeping disabled for this microbench. It measures import (including network) separately from initialization plus world creation. Defaults differ between engines, so this is a screening test, not a fair solver-quality ranking. Browser timer granularity is 0.1 ms; many tiny steps quantize to zero. Same desktop CPU at both viewport sizes. No WebGL workload in the microbench. Own solver remains unmeasured because no comparable implementation exists.

The interactive spike uses six fitted convex slices for the rifle and a simplified eight-sided convex shoulder/cone plus a handle for each top; 120 Hz simulation, at most 50 ms catch-up per rendered frame, 16 solver iterations, gravity 9.81, restitution .28 above 1 unit/s and zero below (to prevent perpetual low-speed bounce), friction .38. These are original tuning choices, not claimed game constants. Its snapshots are diagnostic evidence, not a final art direction. It deliberately uses meshes so physics can be judged before adding splat sorting cost. A static 200-unit visual plane covers the camera view; the collision plane is mathematically infinite. Only a bounded patch is interactive.

## World model

**Coordinates.** Keep the existing right-handed three frame: X right, Y up, +Z toward the viewer. Ground is Y=0. The stage key is at (-.3,3,3.5), so its floor shadow direction is normalize(.3,0,-3.5), approximately (.0854,0,-.9963). Use that vector as the depth basis, with a perpendicular horizontal basis. The current cat shadow is an oval texture under him, not the projection of that key: no physically derived angle should be inferred from the oval alone. Re-anchor it on the shared plane and offset by light direction × height; keep its soft felt character. The spike grid is a ruler for review; hide it in the live page.

**Camera and lower floor.** Retain stage's 30° perspective, near-frontal view, `lookAt(0,.5,0)` and rise framing. Do not tilt to a bird's-eye view just to make dragging easy. Choose a foreground floor patch whose projection lies below today's feet+12px line, with a target near 72–80% of usable height and at least 24px clearance above footer controls. Solve its front/back depth from screen rays at layout time; never divide by a near-parallel ray at the horizon. The page floor has no visible front edge. The spike retains the existing camera formula and uses a view offset to put the origin at min(76% viewport height, height−140px). Production uses the actual footer insets rather than that prototype reserve. The phone view exposes how much compression the shallow angle creates.

**One world, 3D art and cached pictures.** Every tangible keepsake has a stable ID, actual 3D geometry or a transform-aware volume/splat, a fitted collider, tuned mass/material and explicit support. Loose items use `free`, `held` and `floor`; supported items use `shelf-slot`, `board-pin`, `rack-peg`, `lamp-socket`, `sky-anchor`, `attached` or `fixed`, with a support ID/local transform. Choreography is a temporary motion owner, not a second competing solver. Splat and volume data are not collision shapes. A cached render of a real 3D object is allowed; a single front image used for every turn is not. DOM remains for controls and accessible reading transcripts, not the visible physical card, hat, tin or box. Grease is the explicit glass-film exception; the puddle is a world-space surface/sensor.

**Occlusion.** Loose props share the foreground patch and actual depth; board/shelf use reserved side zones. Mesh faces, edges, pins and supports occlude each other; nearest visible geometry wins picking, including a card partly covering the tiny pat. Sky art starts behind the cat, but lifting the cloud/star into the foreground changes its physical depth and picking together. The cached cat/splat pictures need explicit depth proxies or ordered compositing at crossings; CSS z-index is not 3D depth. Keep foreground separation where a proxy is insufficient; never accept a selected rear object through a visible front object. Unit 5 establishes the shared rule before independent furniture work.

**Cat.** He remains a floating cached splat picture, not a simulated ragdoll. Keep the existing picture invalidation and on-cat hat/butter pass; unit 7 supplies shaped hats to that pass and unit 8b supplies shaped eyes. He may have a simple interaction volume for returning butter or attaching eyes; no body mass/collision impulse should displace his authored emotes. Floor shadows refer to world coordinates, never to draggable object-local planes. For meshes use one bounded directional shadow map. For splats use an invisible mesh shadow proxy with matching world transform, or a cached projected silhouette; splats do not automatically cast correct stock three shadow maps.

**Motion ownership.** `world.step` owns transform while a prop is dynamic; egg choreography owns a kinematic body temporarily, then hands off position, rotation and velocity exactly once. Existing top's authored six-second lean and new rigid orientation must not both write the same object. Fixed dt accumulator, bounded pointer-target speed, no huge resume step after tab hiding. Sleeping physics unregisters from stage.onFrame; no step, transform scan, DOM layout, texture upload, sort or shadow/picture redraw while idle. Attached geometry inherits its parent/cache transform; no new polling loop follows the cat. Bounded interaction effects unregister when settled. The live cat's float/stage still render today: zero new world work is not a promise the entire existing page stops. The isolated spike can stop all rAF calls because it has no floating cat.

**Grabbing and placement.** Raycast visible art, transform hit into collider-body local coordinates, create point joint to collisionless anchor; constrain translation at that point only. Release removes joint and retains solver velocity. Tap-versus-drag threshold preserves top-spin/rifle-hitmarker actions. Camera-facing drag plane makes lift predictable; mouse wheel changes depth while held. Production touch gets a deliberate depth mode (two-finger vertical slide or a visible floor placement preview), not a surprise axis switch. Snap candidates use projected distance AND support compatibility/depth, never screen overlap alone. Capture/cancel/blur must release the joint and wake bodies. Escape cancels, keyboard offers lift/place controls. Prototype is a physics study, not the keyboard-accessible final UI.

**Persistence.** Save position/quaternion, support/slot ID and item state when asleep or attached, flush on pagehide; do not write localStorage every simulation tick. Migrate v2 x/y saves onto the foreground patch quietly, retain creation order and finds, clamp to new viewport without resetting discovery. Restore sleeping poses with no sound or bounce; preserve an exact valid pose at the same settled viewport, correcting only a real layout change or unreachable position. Resize remaps placements to reachable floor/slots, never changes physical scale mid-grab. Save schema version separately from the grimoire's discovery schema; storage failure must not break play.

**Reading the specification.** Masses below are starting *play weights*, not kilograms or a density claim; they preserve the current rifle 1.20 / top .20 relationship. Tune contact, inertia and damping against the checks before changing shared solver settings. `D` means natural rigid-body sleep after a bounded interaction/drop, within 8 simulation seconds, followed by cached art/shadow and no adapter callback; unsupported bodies must not be forcibly slept in midair to pass. `S` means a supported item finishes its damped settle, stores the local pose, disables its dynamic solver participation and retains a fixed collision/pick representation; lifting restores its mass and free body. Floor-resting bodies remain collision-wakeable. Fixed mass 0 denotes furniture/sensor/choreography support, never a “weightless” loose prop.

All **Own** entries mean original modeled geometry and deterministic procedural material/print sources committed with seeds, dimensions, texture sizes and build method in the owning unit's `assets/world/<unit>/SOURCES.md`. **Cards must remain readable.** Use deterministic front/back print textures with sufficient texels for the largest supported view, mipmaps and suitable anisotropy; bake/upload only when content, font readiness or required resolution changes. Thickness/curl must show in an edge/oblique view without putting the text on an inflated box. Aerodynamic drag/torque acts only in flight and fades at contact; sheets settle broad face down/up on Y=0, not standing upright to fake readability. Pinning is a single top-point constraint with a small hanging swing (target <=8 degrees, settled within 2s); its final board pose faces the viewer. Unpinning restores dynamics once, without a pose jump.

At **1280×900 and 390×844**, the default board/tray presentation must make the identifying print (“REJECTED”, “THE VOID”, “BUTTAMAN”) readable at >=12 CSS px projected glyph height; use larger slots or a selectable front card on phone rather than shrinking text. Paper's reader, postcard's front/back flip and ticket's encore tap remain; ticket Read is a separate labeled action. Check every face at actual viewport size, including 200% text size and keyboard focus return. **Confirmed hats (e1110-37); sky and small bodies for veto.** Hats keep pixel motifs on physically shaped cones/brims/bands, with side/back/underside detail and material lighting; no billboard or extrusion of a flat sprite silhouette. The same model transfers between peg, grip, floor and cat, and butter sits on its true crown height. Crumbs and shards each use one grouped body by default, not many tiny independently solved particles. Preserve their tap hop/clink as a bounded physical impulse.

The cloud drifts for at most one existing 26s drift cycle after creation, storm or return-to-sky, decelerates to a saved sky anchor, then sleeps; no recurring wake timer. Pulling it down releases that support and makes it a light, damped handled/falling body. A slow release into a visible sky target, or keyboard “return to sky,” restores support and a new finite drift; a floor release stays down.
