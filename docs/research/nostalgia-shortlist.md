# Nostalgia shortlist
The Easter eggs to build first on the floating-cat page: Aaron's six brainstorm picks (decision e1107-39) plus two of round 2's first five that still earn a place, each a mini-spec a builder can start from.
Research round 2, 2026-11-07, revised for the turntable (e1107-36) and an iPhone as the test phone (e1107-38). Hours assume Claude writes the code and a hobbyist tests and tunes it; Blender time is listed separately.

**Words used below.** An `emote_*` clip is a full interrupt: it takes over the cat's body and ends on a named pose (`returnTo`), then the pose cycle carries on. An `add_*` clip is a small overlay on top of whatever pose he is in. Both are wired in `CONFIG.cycle.emotes` and `CONFIG.cycle.actions` in the cat-viewer, which already has `add_hop`, `add_startle`, `emote_wake`, `emote_spin` and `emote_flinch`; a new emote is one table entry plus its clip. "Spin" is the turntable: a sideways drag turns him around the vertical axis only (`yaw`, with flick speed in `vel.x`). "Sound pool" is the Web Audio player, which respects the iPhone silent switch (e1107-37); Butterman switches the session to `playback` so his gag plays anyway.

**One change the picks need first: split the gestures by where the finger lands** (about 1.5 h, counted under pick 1). Today the whole-window canvas spins him from a drag anywhere, and it has `touch-action: pan-y`, which hands vertical swipes to the browser. When the browser takes a gesture it fires `pointercancel` and the page gets no more points ([MDN: pointercancel](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/pointercancel_event)), so any sigil with an up-down stroke would be cut off. The page never scrolls (`body { overflow: hidden }`), so set the canvas to `touch-action: none` and route each press at `pointerdown` with the raycast that `tap()` already uses. A press on the cat (or on his generous hit area) is a spin, a shake or a 360. A press on empty space that barely moves is a background tap (pick 8). Anything longer that starts on empty space is a sigil stroke (pick 1). Fallback, so a sideways swipe beside him still spins him: if the stroke matches no sigil and is a nearly straight sideways line, fling him with its speed on release.

## 1. Sigil spells
**Trigger:** draw one stroke on the empty background with a finger or the mouse (see the gesture split above). **What happens:** a fading ink line follows the finger on a 2D overlay canvas (`pointer-events: none`) above the 3D one. On release, if the stroke is longer than about 120 px, the $1 Unistroke Recognizer compares it with the sigil templates: it resamples the stroke to 64 points, scales it to a square, centres it, and tries rotations within ±45°. $1 always names a best match, so the score is the gate: 0.80 or more casts the spell, the ink flashes once (a single fade, not a strobe) and sinks into the cat. Anything lower crumbles into dust with a "fizzle" sound. Starting spells (a `CONFIG.sigils` table: template name → action): **circle** → `emote_spin`; **zig-zag** → zap (`emote_flinch` plus a crackle sound); **star** → hole in the universe (pick 2); **triangle** → loaf now (pick 3); a custom **B** → summon Butterman. $1 ships 16 templates (triangle, x, rectangle, circle, check, caret, zig-zag, arrow, both square brackets, v, delete, both curly braces, star, pigtail); new ones such as the B are recorded in its demo page and pasted in as a point list. **Discovery:** a "GRIMOIRE" badge in the footer (pick 6) opens a small card with two sigils drawn and the rest as "???". **Hooks:** the gesture split, the emote table, Butterman, the sound pool. **Cost:** about 4.5 h of code (1.5 h of it is the gesture split; the recognizer is one file of about 350 lines, used as-is); no Blender. **Riffs on:** the gesture miracles of [Black & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game)) (Lionhead, 2001), where the team "avoided using control panels, icons and buttons for casting miracles, preferring a gesture system"; the engine is the [$1 Unistroke Recognizer](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/index.html) (Wobbrock, Wilson and Li, UIST 2007).
**Needs from Aaron:** pick which shape fires which spell; a fizzle sound and a zap crackle; draw the B once in the $1 demo so its template is his handwriting.

## 2. Hole in the universe
**Trigger:** the star sigil, or keep him spinning fast: flick him again and again so his spin stays above about 8 rad/s (roughly 900 px/s of finger) for 2 s in total. **What happens:** his matte-black body stops being a cat-shaped object and becomes a cat-shaped window onto space. A starfield flies outward from the centre of the screen, seen only through his silhouette; his rim light disappears, so he reads as a cut-out in the page. While you keep him spinning, the warp speed follows his spin speed; when he slows below the threshold the stars fade back to matte black over 1 s. From the star sigil it lasts 6 s. Butter, if he has it, stays solid and floats on the hole, which is the joke. A low "whummm" plays while it is open. **How to draw it (cheap path):** not a stencil. Patch the cat's own material (`onBeforeCompile`) so that, while a uniform `hole` is above 0, it ignores lighting and paints a procedural starfield computed from the screen position (`gl_FragCoord`) and time: a few layers of hashed points moving outward. It is one extra branch in the shader the cat already draws, no extra pass, and the work only happens on the pixels the cat covers. It works the same on the skinned GLB, because the patch rides on whatever material three.js already compiles for skinning. **Hooks:** the cat material, `vel.x`, the sigil table. **Cost:** about 3.5 h of code (including a check in the cat-glb prototype); no Blender. **Riffs on:** the Windows "Starfield Simulation" screensaver (Windows 3.1 to XP): stars appear at the centre and stream outward, with a warp-speed slider and 10 to 200 stars. [Colin Mitchell rebuilt it and explains how it works](https://muffinlabs.com/screensavers/2-stars/). Here, his spin is the warp slider.
**Needs from Aaron:** the "whummm" sound; whether it should also open on its own very rarely.

## 3. Loaf → actual loaf
**Trigger:** none from the visitor. When he settles into `lie` (the loaf pose in the cat-viewer), there is a 15% chance, at most once every 2 minutes, that 1.5 s into the hold he becomes bread. The triangle sigil forces it. **What happens:** a quick squash-and-pop puff (0.15 s), the cat hides, and a brown loaf of bread sits exactly where he was, gently bobbing with the float. A toaster "ding". It holds for one beat (1.2 s, or one beat of Butterman's BPM grid), then puffs back into the cat in the same pose. If he is buttered, the butter moves to the top of the loaf and back, which makes Butterman's whole plan make sense. Tapping the loaf plays a soft "boop" and turns him back at once. Never during a transition, an emote or a Butterman scene. **Hooks:** the pose cycle (a hook on entering a hold), butter (re-parent the slab), tap, `CONFIG.loaf = { chance: 0.15, after: 1.5, hold: 1.2, cooldown: 120 }`. **Cost:** about 2 h of code with a loaf built in code (`RoundedBoxGeometry` is already imported: a rounded box with a domed top, a warm brown material and scored lines painted on a canvas texture); add about 1 h if he wants a modelled loaf. [Kenney's Food Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/food-kit) is CC0 (free to use, no credit needed) but I did not confirm it has a loaf. **Riffs on:** the cat loaf, the internet's name for a cat sitting with its paws tucked under; [Know Your Meme files "The Catloaf"](https://knowyourmeme.com/photos/917712-cats) under its Cats entry (2015).
**Needs from Aaron:** the ding and boop; how rare it should be.

## 4. 360 no-scope
**Trigger:** one swipe on the cat that turns him one full turn, then lets him stop. From `pointerdown` to the moment the spin dies down (speed below 0.3 rad/s, before the drift home starts), his total turn must be between 330° and 450°, all in one direction (no reversals in the drag). **What happens:** two seconds of MLG mode. One air-horn blast. Three hitmarker crosses tick onto his body with a tick sound each. Pixel sunglasses drop onto his face. A spinning plain nacho chip orbits him once. A caption in big bold condensed type: "OH BABY A TRIPLE". Then `add_hop`, and everything slides off-screen. No flashing colours: Know Your Meme warns that the montages themselves are "flashy and loud". Cooldown 20 s. **Why it is a real skill on a phone:** the drag turns him 0.009 rad per pixel, so a full-width swipe on an iPhone (about 390 px) only turns him about 200°. The rest has to come from the flick's coast, which at the prototype's damping adds about speed ÷ 3.5 radians. A release at about 10 rad/s (roughly 1,100 px/s, a brisk flick) finishes the turn. That is easy to do by accident sometimes and hard to do on purpose every time, which suits a secret. **Hooks:** `endPress`, `vel.x`, the coast in the frame loop, `CONFIG.rotate.returnDelay` (detection must end before the drift home starts), the sound pool. **Cost:** about 2.5 h of code; no Blender (the sunglasses and chip are 2D). **Riffs on:** [montage parodies](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/montage-parodies) (YouTube, from 2011), which spoof gaming highlight reels with air horns, the Call of Duty hitmarker sound, Doritos jokes and the "360 no scope" trick shot.
**Needs from Aaron:** an air horn and a hitmarker tick he records or synthesizes himself (the real hitmarker sound is Activision's); keep the chip unbranded (Doritos is a trademark).

## 5. Yell to summon Butterman
**Trigger:** tap a small megaphone button (next to Buttaman), then yell at the phone within 6 s. **What happens:** the tap asks for the microphone (`getUserMedia`, asking for `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` to be off, so loud stays loud). A little Win98-style volume meter appears with "YELL!". Every frame, an `AnalyserNode` measures the loudness of the last ~20 ms. The first 0.5 s sets a baseline for the room; a yell is 20 dB above it, held for 0.25 s. The peak picks Butterman's rise size: loud = stage 1, louder = stage 2, loudest = stage 3, which matches the three widening "BUTTAMAN!" stages of his rise scene (`buttaman-1/2/3.wav`). Then, in this order: stop every mic track, wait about 0.5 s, set the audio session to `playback`, and play the Butterman gag. No yell in 6 s: the mic closes and the meter says "too quiet". **Hooks:** Butterman's scene (an entry point that takes a size), the existing `setAudioSession`, the shared `AudioContext`. **Cost:** about 3.5 h of code plus about 1 h of testing on the iPhone against the deployed site (see Shaky parts: it can't be tested on the phone over the home Wi-Fi). **Riffs on:** [Nintendogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendogs) (Nintendo DS, 2005), where you call your dog by speaking its name into the DS microphone.
**Needs from Aaron:** a yes that iPhone visitors will see Safari's microphone prompt every visit (see Shaky parts); a "too quiet" sound.

## 6. Footer as a 1999 web page
**Trigger:** none; it is the footer the brother asked for. **What happens:** one strip along the bottom, drawn in our style (matte black on cream, one accent colour). **88×31 buttons** for the social links, plus a "GRIMOIRE" one (pick 1) and a "Powered by Butter" one. Each is drawn as a CSS or pixel-font badge with the network's name in text, not its logo pasted into a GIF. At 88×31 CSS px each, four fit across a 390 px iPhone. Each gets an invisible hit area at least 44 pt tall, Apple's minimum tap target, since 31 px is too small for a thumb. **An odometer counter** in black digit tiles. It counts bonks (taps on the cat) on this device: "YOU'VE BONKED HIM #000037 TIMES". That folds in round 2's bonk counter: milestones at 10, 100, 500 and 1,000 get a pun title ("ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED: ONE THOUSMEOW") and `emote_spin`, and each tap has a 1-in-50 **rare bonk** (a rare sound, a "RARE BONK" caption, `add_startle`). Storage is `localStorage`, every read and write in try/catch. **A webring line:** "← prev | sigil ring | next →", linking the brothers' own pages. **Small print:** "Best viewed with a cat on your keyboard at 800×600", an "under construction" cat in a hard hat next to anything unfinished, and "Last updated: whenever the cat felt like it". A real shared visitor count and a guestbook need a small backend (the brainstorm's free Cloudflare Worker), so they wait. **Hooks:** `tap()` (it already counts `taps`), the emote table, the sound pool, the footer element. **Cost:** about 4 h of code (2 h of it is the bonk counter); no Blender. **Riffs on:** [The 88x31 GIF Collection](https://cyber.dabamos.de/88x31/) (4,540 classic buttons from the 1990s on, collected by a hobbyist who asks people not to hot-link them), the webring, and [nyan.cat](https://www.nyan.cat)'s counter and pun achievements ("ONE THOUSNYAN", "MISSINGNYAN").
**Needs from Aaron:** the social accounts; the counter verb (bonked, petted, booped?); 2–3 rare bonk sounds; which friends' sites go in the ring.

## 7. Kept: Shake him silly
Kept because it is the cheapest gag on the list, it rewards the main control, and the turntable doesn't hurt it. It stays separate from the other spin gags: the 360 is one turn in one direction, and the hole needs fast spinning kept up, while a shake is rapid reversals.

**Trigger:** shake the cat hard while dragging him sideways: four or more sharp left-right direction changes, each faster than about 1,500 px a second, within 0.8 s. It works the same with a finger or a mouse, so phones need no motion-sensor permission. **What happens:** he stops fighting it. The pose cycle is interrupted by `emote_dizzy` (about 2.5 s: head circles, ears flat, a wobble, then a slump), which ends on `lie`. Three small black stars orbit his head for the length of the clip (three.js sprites parented to the head), and one long woozy "mrrrraaaow" plays. If he is buttered, the butter pops off with the slump. Cooldown 8 s, so a long drag cannot loop it. The spin keeps working the whole time: the clip moves his body, the drag turns his `yaw`. **Hooks:** the drag code (it already tracks a smoothed flick speed in `vel.x`), the emote table (one new entry plus the shake detector), the sound pool, butter. **Cost:** about 1.5 h of code; 1–2 h in Blender for `emote_dizzy` on the real model. **Riffs on:** [Staggering Beauty](http://www.staggeringbeauty.com) (George Michael Brower, 2012): a calm black worm that loses it when you shake the mouse. Keep the calm-to-chaos surprise; drop the strobe and screaming that now sit behind its seizure warning.
**Needs from Aaron:** one dizzy sound clip (2–3 s).

## 8. Kept: Pointer Cat (he watches you)
Kept because it gives background taps a meaning, and the gesture split makes them cleanly different from sigil strokes (a tap barely moves; a stroke does). The same head turn is reused when sigils cast and when Butterman walks in. The rare bonk from the old bonk counter moved into the footer (pick 6).

**Trigger:** tap or click empty background, not the cat. Today that does nothing (`tap()` returns false on a miss); this answers round 1's open question 5. **What happens:** his head snaps round to stare at the spot. A small paw-print marker appears there and fades within 1 s. He holds the stare for 1.2 s, then eases back. Spam-tap around the screen and he whips back and forth trying to keep up; five misses within 2 s gets an `emote_huff` (ending on `sit`) and a short annoyed "mrrp". On desktop, between taps, his head lazily follows the cursor; on touch there is no hover, so he only reacts to taps. The head turn is code, not a clip: after `mixer.update()` each frame, turn the `head` part (a bone named `head` on the real model) toward the target, worked out in the cat's own frame so it still works whichever way he has been spun, limited to ±60° side to side and ±35° up and down, and eased. If the spot is behind him, he turns his head as far as the limit allows and looks annoyed. Asleep, he ignores background taps except for an `add_ear_twitch`. This is round 1's Neko-eyes idea minus the idle-to-sleep timer, which the pose cycle already covers. **Hooks:** the tap raycast miss, the per-frame loop after the mixer, the emote player, the pose cycle (whether he is asleep). **Cost:** about 3 h of code (the turntable means he is never upside down, which keeps the maths simple); 0.5–1 h in Blender for `emote_huff`, plus an optional 0.5 h for `add_ear_twitch`. The real model must have a bone named `head`. **Riffs on:** [Pointer Pointer](https://pointerpointer.com) (Studio Moniker, 2012), where a photo of someone pointing at your cursor appears wherever you stop, so the page seems to see you; and [Neko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_(software)) (1989), the desktop cat that watches the cursor.
**Needs from Aaron:** one "mrrp" clip, and a yes to using background taps this way.

**Totals.** Code: about 25.5 h for all eight on the prototype cat (sigils 4.5, hole 3.5, loaf 2, 360 2.5, yell 4.5 including phone testing, footer 4, shake 1.5, Pointer Cat 3). The `emote_*` player already exists, so none of these pay for it. Blender for the real model: about 2.5–4 h (`emote_dizzy`, `emote_huff`, the optional `add_ear_twitch`, and the optional modelled loaf). Sounds from Aaron: about 14 short clips (fizzle, zap, whummm, ding, boop, air horn, hitmarker tick, too quiet, dizzy, mrrp, 2–3 rare bonks); Butterman's own clips are already planned. Build order: the gesture split and sigils first (they decide how every touch is routed), then footer, Pointer Cat, shake, 360, loaf, hole, yell. The yell goes last because it needs the deployed site to test.

## Shaky parts (checked against primary sources)
- **The mic on iPhone Safari prompts on every visit.** WebKit resets camera and microphone permission whenever the user reloads or navigates: a WebKit engineer's 2024 change keeps resetting it "in case user decided for the navigation (whether same origin or not) or the reload" ([WebKit PR #35948](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/pull/35948)). An iPhone user reports that the prompt has no "always allow" ([WebKit bug 280394](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=280394)). The only way to stop it is Safari's per-site setting (aA → Website Settings → Microphone → Allow). For an egg the visitor opts into with a button, one prompt per visit is fine; it rules out any always-listening mic.
- **The mic changes how sound plays.** Calling `getUserMedia` switches iOS to the "play-and-record" audio category. That lowers playback volume and can send it to the earpiece ([WebKit bug 218012](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=218012), [bug 236219](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=236219)). MDN's Audio Session page says a mic track defaults to `play-and-record` and may move output "to the earpiece instead of the speaker" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Audio_Session_API)). A developer who shipped a voice mode found that iOS "clung to play-and-record for several hundred milliseconds" after the tracks closed ([Sam Eddy, 2026](https://samueleddy.com/writing/ios-safari-audio-sessions/)). Hence pick 5's order: listen with nothing playing, stop every track, wait about 0.5 s, then switch to `playback` for Butterman. Don't set `playback` before asking for the mic: one practitioner reports that WebKit then refuses capture ([Lightning Jar](https://www.lightningjar.com/blog/web-audio-wont-tell-you); a single source, so test it). A WebKit bug from iOS 18.6 says turning off `autoGainControl` and the other processing does not fix the routing (bug 218012). Whether Safari honours `autoGainControl: false` at all is unverified, which is why the trigger compares against the room's baseline instead of a fixed level.
- **The mic needs HTTPS, so it can't be tested over the home Wi-Fi.** `getUserMedia` only exists in a secure context ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia)). GitHub Pages serves HTTPS, and `localhost` counts as secure on the computer. But the iPhone opening `http://192.168.x.x:8000` from the computer is not secure, so the mic simply isn't there. Test the yell on the deployed site, or through an HTTPS tunnel. I didn't confirm whether Safari also demands a tap before `getUserMedia`; the megaphone button supplies one either way.
- **Silent switch.** Tap sounds stay `ambient` and respect it (e1107-37). The yell's Butterman gag uses Butterman's existing `playback` switch, so it plays on silent like the regular Buttaman button.
- **The starfield: a stencil is the expensive way.** In three.js 0.186.1 (the pinned version) `WebGLRenderer` is created with `stencil = false` by default (checked in its source). A stencil version means turning that on when the renderer is built, drawing the cat into the stencil, then drawing a full-screen starfield pass masked by it: an extra pass every frame while the hole is open. The material patch in pick 2 draws nothing extra and only shades the cat's pixels, so it is the one to build. Check it on the iPhone with `?perf=1`, which already shows fps and draw calls.
- **Sigils against spin and scroll.** `touch-action: pan-y` (in the cat-viewer today) gives up-down movement to the browser, which then fires `pointercancel` and ends the stroke ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/pointercancel_event)). With a page that never scrolls, `touch-action: none` on the canvas loses nothing. If the footer ever grows into a scrolling section, scroll it on its own element, not on the canvas.
- **What $1 can't tell apart.** It stretches every stroke to a square, and its source says plainly that this "assumes 2D gestures (i.e., no lines)". So a straight line can't be a sigil, which is why a straight sideways swipe falls back to a spin. It compares points in drawing order (`PathDistance` pairs point *i* with point *i*), so a circle drawn the other way round may not match: add a mirrored template for each sigil. It only searches ±45° of rotation. It always returns its best guess, so the 0.80 score gate does the rejecting. If direction keeps tripping people up, the same lab's $P recognizer "ignores stroke number, order, and direction".
- **The $1 licence is fine.** The JavaScript is under the New BSD License: "Copyright (C) 2007-2012, Jacob O. Wobbrock, Andrew D. Wilson and Yang Li" ([source header, dollar.js](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/dollar.js); the [project page](https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/index.html) says the same). It allows use in our site if we keep the copyright notice and licence text in the file, and don't use the University of Washington's or Microsoft's names to endorse the site. Vendor the file unchanged, header and all.
- **360 detection is a tuning question.** It depends on `radiansPerPixel`, `damping` and the drift home; change any of those and the window moves. Expose the 330–450° window in `CONFIG` and tune it on the iPhone.

- **neal.fun.** Route: Exa fetch of the HTML. Saw only "Neal.fun / Contact"; the pages build themselves in JavaScript, and Stimulation Clicker gave only "Click me / 0 stimulation / Upgrades".
